The business question

Brightwater Retail runs 20 stores in Ireland. In the second half of 2025, net revenue fell against the same period of 2024. Management asked three questions:

  1. Why did revenue fall?
  2. Where did the problem start: which stores, categories, products and suppliers?
  3. What should we do about it, and how would we spot it sooner next time?

The hard part is not finding a number that moved. It is separating what the data proves from what it only suggests, so that money is spent fixing real causes rather than guesses.

Key findings

Observed changecause: unresolved

Three stores account for over half of the decline

Stores S03, S04 and S05 lost about 27.8% of their transactions, against 1.1% for matched controls. A difference-in-differences estimate puts the excess decline at €397,778 (54% of the total), range €377,245 to €435,210. Footfall, competitor and store-operations data were not available, so the cause is not established.

Root-cause evidencecause: established

Electronics stock-outs cost measurable sales

Electronics & Tech availability fell from 95.4% to 89.3%, mostly on Voltix-supplied products. Estimated lost sales were about €37,235 gross and €23,963 net (range €23,963 to €44,460). Whether the supplier or the retailer's ordering is responsible is not established.

Observed changecause: not established

“Faulty” returns rose, but that does not prove defects

Refunds recorded with the till reason “Faulty” rose by €31,589 (+90%), concentrated in five stores. A till reason is not a verified defect; QA and batch data would be needed.

Statistical associationcause: not established

Home & Kitchen promotions are associated with the decline, not proven to cause it

The discount rate rose from about 2.4% to 11.9%. Four estimation methods disagree in sign, so they are reported separately and never added together.

Financial size sets investigation priority; evidence strength sets what action is justified.
